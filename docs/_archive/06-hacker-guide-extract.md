Welcome to HackGlobal
HackGlobal is a global online hackathon by Polaris School of Technology, co-powered by
Vultr, with builders from 28 countries and 7 partner universities. The kickoff is on the
evening of 13 November, and hacking runs for 36 hours, from 00:00 IST on 14 November to
12:00 IST on 15 November 2026. This guide has everything you need to build, get help and
submit.

Teams

Finding teammates: post in the team-formation channel on Discord with your role,
skills and time zone.
Cross-university teams: teams with members from different universities get bonus
points, which improves your odds in the final round.
Non-technical builders welcome: designers, product thinkers and domain experts
are valuable. You don't need a CS background.
Team changes: teams are locked now that hacking has started, so no changes are
possible.

Vultr credits and setup
Every participant gets $200 in Vultr credits, free. Use them for Compute instances,
Managed Databases, Kubernetes clusters or any other Vultr service during the
hackathon.
Before you start: use an email address that has never been linked to a Vultr account. Use
the same account for Vultr Community so everything stays in one place.
1. Create a Vultr account. Go to the Vultr registration page, enter your email and a
strong password, accept the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, and click Create
Free Account.
2. Verify your email. Open the email from Vultr and click Verify Your E-mail. You'll land in
the Vultr Console.
3. Join Vultr Community and check in (see How to submit).
4. Claim your credits. Fill the Vultr credits form to get your $200 in Vultr credits.
Your credits and account stay active through the event. To keep your deployments
running after that, add a payment method in the Billing section.
Getting started with your first deployment: follow the Deploy on Vultr guide in the starter
kit.
Questions about Vultr? Ask in the Discord server.

Starter kit (GitHub)
The HackGlobal starter kit gives you ready-to-use templates so you can start building in
minutes. Link: hackglobal-starter on GitHub.
1. Open the starter kit repo and click Use this template to make your own copy.
2. Pick the template folder that fits your project.
3. Follow the README in that folder to run it locally.
4. Deploy it on Vultr using the deploy guide in the repo.
What's inside: three templates (an AI chat app, a web research agent using Exa, and a
Streamlit demo), a step-by-step Vultr deploy guide, a project README template and a
submission checklist. Using it is optional. You can build in any stack.
To run the templates you need an AI API key from any OpenAI-compatible provider. The
starter kit's README explains the options.

1. Open community.vultr.com and click the profile icon in the top-right corner.

After you submit: a moderator reviews your project. Once approved, it appears on the
Project Showcase page and the HackGlobal 2026 hackathon page. Standout projects may
be featured.
Submit early. No submissions are accepted after the deadline. All deadlines are in IST.
Tips to finish strong
Keep your idea small. One feature that works beats five that don't.
Deploy on Vultr by Saturday evening, not in the last hour.
Test your live link in a private browser window before you submit.
Submit at least an hour before the 12:00 IST deadline on Sunday.
Judging and prizes
HackGlobal has a total prize pool of $15,000, including $2,500 in cash and $2,500 in cool
tech gadgets, plus credits and more.
What judges look for
Merit: how well the project solves a real problem, and the quality of the build
Reach: real users you reached, plus likes, shares and votes on your project
Bonus points: cross-university teams, pre-event challenges, community participation
Prize rules
Each team can win only one prize, either a placement or one special award.
Internship opportunities are separate, and any team can get one.
Demo Day
Six finalist teams present live on Demo Day, Sun 15 Nov, 21:30 to 23:30 IST. Each team gets
7 minutes to present, plus 1 minute of questions from the judges. Winners are announced
at the end of the stream.
Optional: LinkedIn idea contest
Post your HackGlobal idea on LinkedIn and tag Polaris School of Technology for a chance
to win AirPods. It's individual and fully optional. Skipping it doesn't affect your HackGlobal
participation or judging.
Post format and deadline: shared on Discord.

Win while you build
You earn points on Discord for being active: introducing yourself, chatting, sharing your
progress and joining sessions. Top members win rewards. We also run fun contests on
Discord and the live stream through the weekend.
Rewards and contest list: shared on Discord.
Rules and code of conduct
Build your project during the hackathon, starting at 00:00 IST on 14 November. You can
plan ideas and designs before, but projects built before the start are not valid
submissions.
Use any language or stack. Open-source libraries, APIs and the starter kit are allowed.
Credit what you use in your README.
AI coding tools are allowed, but your project must be your team's own work. Copied,
reused or pre-built projects will be disqualified, with or without AI.
No plagiarism or copied projects. Projects found copied will be disqualified.
You own what you build.
Be respectful to everyone. Harassment of any kind is not tolerated.
To report a problem, message an organizer on Discord or email
events@polariscampus.com.
By taking part, you agree to follow the rules in the Discord server and a general hackathon
code of conduct: be respectful, be honest, and help keep HackGlobal safe and welcoming
for everyone.
FAQ
Can I take part without a technical background? Yes. Designers, product thinkers and
domain experts are welcome.
Can I build solo? Yes, solo participation is allowed.
How big can my team be? Teams can have 1 to 5 members.
Which tech stack can I use? Any language, framework or tool. Deploy your project on
Vultr.
I'm in a different time zone. Will I miss things? No. The website schedule shows every
session in your local time, and the kickoff is replayed on Sat 14 Nov, 05:40 to 07:30 IST.
Where do I submit my project? On the HackGlobal page on Vultr Community, from 15
November. Not on HackCulture.
